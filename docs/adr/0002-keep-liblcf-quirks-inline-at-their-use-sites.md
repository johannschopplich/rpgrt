# Keep liblcf quirks inline at their use sites

A central `quirks.ts` gathering the non-CSV liblcf facts (the Terms omission set, the Parameters stride, the version-zero rule, the is2k3 write-back, the detection sample keys) was considered and rejected. The quirks share nothing but "not in the CSVs": most are predicates or values derived from the generated descriptors, not movable data, and each has exactly one consumer with its why-comment sitting beside the branch – centralizing the data while the branches stay put would split every quirk across two files. `docs/serialization.md` §10 is already the index of what rpgrt special-cases, with the liblcf C++ citations code cannot carry; a code-level mirror would be a fourth artifact to keep in sync and could only cover the subset that happens to be a literal. The one genuine duplication, the header-preserving record list shared between the build-time generator and `LCF_FORMATS`, is pinned by a generator test instead.

Deleting the `berIntList` codec kind as "unreachable" was rejected with it. The kind is the codec for the CSV's single `DBArray<Int32>` row (`EventCommand.parameters`): it drives the generated `parameters: number[]` type the translation layer consumes, gives `defaultRecord` its `[]`, and its two throw arms in the engine are the guard that a DBArray never appears as a chunk payload – EventCommand serializes through the hand-coded raw path. Removing it would either break the generated interface or force mapping to `vector<int32>`, whose fixed-width wire form would silently mis-decode a future DBArray chunk field instead of throwing.
